The spec sheet

Rules, not vibes.

Sketchie publishes the research behind its generator, graded by evidence. These are constraints the output has to satisfy, not promises about what a video does to a viewer.

Scene length12 to 30s

One idea per scene, long enough to land and short enough to hold.

Elements per scene3 to 7

Counted at the end of the scene, heading included.

Reveal spacing2 to 5s

A new element arrives on that rhythm, never two closer than 1.2 seconds.

Word sync500ms

Each element appears within that window of the word it belongs to.

Narration140 to 160 wpm

Spoken register, contractions allowed, read at a human pace.

Labels3 words max

A keyword, never a sentence echoing the narration.

Question

Every video closes by asking the viewer to produce the answer.

Pause

The video holds, so the answer is theirs before it is given.

Recap

Then one line closes the loop. The question is never replaced by a summary.

The six minute ceiling comes from engagement research across 6.9 million online learning sessions, and is a deliberate cap rather than a technical limit. Longer material becomes a chaptered series. Read the full spec with citations.

Questions

The honest FAQ.

Can I edit a single scene?

Yes. Scenes are addressable, so an edit targets one of them, that scene re-renders on its own, every edit creates a version, and you can revert to an earlier one.

What if the source is too long?

Longer material becomes a chaptered series rather than one long video. Individual videos are set in 30 second increments up to six minutes, or Sketchie picks the length.

How does a video end?

With a retrieval beat: a question, a pause, then a one line recap. The pause is deliberate, because producing the answer yourself is the part the research supports.

Can I try the editor without paying?

Yes. Two watermarked videos, up to one minute each, no card required, and the editor is included rather than held back for paid plans.

Is Sketchie faster than Simi?

No, and Sketchie says so. Simi is faster at first generation. The argument is about the time it takes to reach a video you will actually publish, revisions included.

Can developers drive it?

Yes. An API that returns the video with its scene graph, an MCP server so agents can generate and edit, and a CLI. API access starts on Plus.
