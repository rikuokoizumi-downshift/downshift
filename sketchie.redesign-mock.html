how it works

Three moves, about a minute.

1

Drop anything in

A prompt, a chapter PDF, your product docs. Sketchie reads the source and writes a script built on story structure, not a bullet list read aloud.

2

Watch it draw

Every element draws on stroke by stroke, timed to the exact word of narration it belongs to. Nothing appears that the narration never mentions.

3

Fix the one scene that is wrong

Open the scene list, change the heading, the labels or the narration, and re-render that scene. The rest of the video stays exactly as it was.

the rules behind the drawing

Built for the person watching it tomorrow.

Sketchie published the research it built the generator on, graded by evidence, including the parts it could not verify. These are rules in the pipeline, not slogans.

The core mechanism

Everything draws on. Always.

Content built up stroke by stroke beats showing a finished diagram, for both retention and transfer. It is the reason the product exists, so the generator never cuts to a completed scene.

Length

Six minutes is a cliff, not a guideline

Across 6.9 million online learning sessions, engagement sits near full under six minutes and collapses past twelve. Longer material becomes a chaptered series.

Endings

Every video ends with a question

A retrieval beat: the question appears, the video pauses, then a one line recap. Making the viewer produce the answer is what makes it stick.

Scenes

One idea, 12 to 30 seconds

Scenes carry three to seven elements and a new reveal every few seconds, with silence held at the boundary so the idea has somewhere to land.

Restraint

No decoration

Every element on screen is referenced by the narration. Interesting but extraneous content counts as a failure in the eval harness, not as flair.

The honest FAQ.

How fast is it, really?

Sketchie describes a full video as taking about a minute, covering the script, the drawings, the narration and the word level timing. Rendering runs in the background, so you are not sitting on a loading screen while it works.

What does an edit cost?

Editing does not consume one of your free videos, and the first edit of each video is free. After that an edit re-renders a single scene rather than regenerating the whole video.

What happens when I go over my minutes?

Nothing, unless you opt in. Overage is off by default, so you stop at your plan ceiling with an upgrade prompt instead of a surprise invoice.

Can my own tools drive it?

Yes. There is an API that returns the video and the editable scene graph, plus an MCP server so an agent can generate and edit explainers directly. API access starts on the Plus plan.

Who is behind it?

Sketchie is operated by Downshift LLC and built by a small team with no venture funding. It launched in July 2026.
