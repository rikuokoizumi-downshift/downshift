The scene model

Fix one scene. Not the whole video.

A Sketchie video is a sequence of addressable scenes. Change the one that is wrong, by editing its fields or describing the change in plain language, and only that scene re-renders.

Illustration of the scene model, using an explainer about DNS

Plain language edit

"In scene 02, name the resolver and show the request leaving the laptop first."

Edits can set fields directly, such as the heading, the narration or a label, or describe a larger change in a sentence.

Version 1original

Two boxes and an arrow. Correct, but the viewer cannot tell who is asking.

Version 2re-rendered

The laptop sends first, the resolver is named, and the answer returns on its own line.

Unchanged Scenes 01, 03 and 04 were not re-rendered. Their timing, narration and artwork are untouched.

AddressableEvery scene has an identity in the scene graph, so an edit can name the one it means.

VersionedEach edit creates a version. Earlier versions stay available, and you can revert to one.

ContainedRe-rendering one scene leaves the rest of the video exactly as it was.

The rules in the generator

Drawn to be followed.

Sketchie publishes the research it built the generator on, graded by evidence, including the parts it could not verify. The numbers below are design rules the output has to satisfy, not claims about what a video will do to a viewer.

Anatomy of one scene, illustrative

Scene duration: 12 to 30 seconds, one idea
reveal 2 to 5s 2 to 5s never closer than 1.2s 3 to 7 elements
Narration 140 to 160 words per minute, each reveal within 500ms of its word
Question

The retrieval beat opens by asking the viewer to produce the answer.

Pause

The video holds, so the answer is theirs before it is given.

One line recap

Then a single line closes the loop. Never a summary in place of the question.

Scene length12 to 30 seconds
Elements per scene3 to 7
Reveal spacing2 to 5 seconds
Minimum gap1.2 seconds
Word syncWithin 500ms
LabelsThree words or fewer

The six minute ceiling comes from engagement research across 6.9 million online learning sessions, which informed a deliberate cap rather than a technical limit. Longer material becomes a chaptered series. Progressive drawing and ending on a question are supported by research into the explanation format itself. Read the full spec, with citations and grades.

Questions

The honest FAQ.

Can I edit a single scene?

Yes. That is the point of the scene graph. Scenes are addressable, so an edit targets one of them, that scene re-renders on its own, every edit creates a version, and you can revert to an earlier one.

What happens when the source is too long?

Longer material becomes a chaptered series rather than one long video. Individual videos are set in 30 second increments up to six minutes, or you can let Sketchie choose the length.

What happens at the end of a video?

Every video ends with a retrieval beat: a question, a pause, then a one line recap. The pause is deliberate, because producing the answer yourself is what the research supports.

Can I try the editor without paying?

Yes. The free tier gives you two watermarked videos, up to one minute each, with no card required, and it includes the editor rather than holding it back for paid plans.

Is Sketchie faster than Simi?

No, and Sketchie says so. Simi is faster at first generation. The argument is about the time it takes to reach a video you will actually publish, revisions included, which is where editable scenes change the arithmetic.

Can developers use it?

Yes. There is an API that returns the video with its scene graph, an MCP server so agents can generate and edit, and a CLI. API access starts on Plus.
