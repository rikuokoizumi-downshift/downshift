Idea 1

A form helper shaped like a window card

In your words: Use a "Form" Format Helper. Functions like a Window Card. Will contain sections with "Scene 1", "How would you like the 1st scene to go?" or something along those lines. We can even use "Intro" directly so people can get a gist of how it is going to be made. Intro, then Scene 1, and on.

Reading it back: Replace the single free-text prompt with a short sequence of named boxes, one per scene, each asking its own small question. The window card is the right reference because the sheet teaches you the task by its shape: you can see every slot before you fill any of them, and you fill them one at a time without having to hold the whole thing in your head.

Why it earns its place: The section names double as a preview. Someone who has never made a video reads Intro, Scene 1, Scene 2, Closing and already knows what they are about to get, which is the part a blank prompt box cannot do. It also stops the blank-page problem, because the first question is small and answerable rather than open.

What it opens:

  • How many scene slots, and can a person add or remove rows, or is the count fixed?
  • Does this replace the prompt box, or sit beside it as a second way in for people who want the rails?
  • Does one filled slot become exactly one scene? If it does, a later edit lands on the right scene on its own, which is the thing the product already sells.
You Idea
Idea 2

Do not keep the editor and the video repository apart

In your words: Don't editor and video repository separately.

Reading it back: The editor and the place finished videos live should be one surface rather than two. Said in shorthand, so this is a reading rather than a quote with the verb filled in, and worth confirming before anything is built on it.

Why it earns its place: Two surfaces means a person finishes a video, leaves the editor, finds it in a library, and has to come back the other way to change anything. Since the whole pitch is that a video stays editable scene by scene, a trip out to a separate library quietly argues the opposite, that the video is done and filed.

What it opens:

  • Does the library open a video in place, or is there no separate library page at all?
  • What happens to a list of twenty videos inside an editor built for one?
You Idea
Idea 3

The reason to open the app is money, not learning

In your words: Portal for students and teachers doesn't work either since it's just going to be another app added to the countless cycle. Instead, focus on making the portal for affiliates. I want my reason to opening the app is to check if I got my money in, or if I want to track my earnings.

Reading it back: A student or teacher portal competes with every other school app for attention and loses, because nothing inside it is urgent. An affiliate portal has the one thing a learning app does not: the person is owed money, and checking whether it arrived is a reason to open the app that comes back on its own without being prompted.

Why it earns its place: It names a habit rather than a feature. Nobody has to be nudged into checking whether they got paid, which is the behaviour engagement features usually try and fail to manufacture. It also picks a user who already wants to bring other people in, so the portal and the distribution channel turn out to be the same thing.

What it opens:

  • What does an affiliate see on day one, before there is anything to be paid? An empty balance is a reason not to come back.
  • Who are the affiliates: teachers referring colleagues, creators with an audience, or a separate paid programme recruited on purpose?
  • Payout cadence decides how often there is genuinely something new to look at. Monthly payouts mean most visits find the same number as last time.
