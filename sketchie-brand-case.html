Design audit and brand case

The product is credible. The brand is not carrying it.

Sketchie publishes a graded research spec, concedes where a competitor wins, and ships output that genuinely draws itself. The visual identity around all of that reads as a classroom worksheet, fails accessibility on most of its palette, and never once shows the thing that makes the product different.

01 / The case in short

Three reasons, none of them taste

Brand arguments usually come down to preference, which is why they stall. These three can be checked by anyone with a browser and a contrast tool.

7 of 9Palette colours fail WCAG AA for body text on the site's own background. Measured, not estimated.
1 of 16Pages carrying any visual of the scene model: a single homepage demo. It appears nowhere in the identity system itself.
$499.99The top published monthly plan, Team. The identity is pitched well below the seriousness of that price.

One, the palette is not usable. Seven of nine brand colours cannot legally carry body text on the warm paper the site uses. In practice that means the palette is decorative only, which is why six accents do almost no work.

Two, the differentiator is a section, not an identity. The homepage carries one demo of the scene list with a re-render row, and that is the only visual expression of it anywhere. No recurring device, no mark, nothing in the system that says a video is made of addressable scenes.

Three, the register is mismatched to the buyer. A marker typeface, hard offset shadows and six bright accents read as a school worksheet. The same site asks developers to buy an API and teams to pay $499 a month.

04 / Why it should change

Five arguments, with the evidence attached

01

The identity never shows the differentiator

Sketchie's defensible claim is that a video is a set of addressable scenes, so the second version costs a scene instead of a rebuild. The homepage does show this once, as a demo block with a scene list and a re-render row. That is where it stops. It is not in the mark, not in the layout system, and not in any device that repeats across pages, so a visitor who skims leaves knowing only that it makes AI videos.

Evidence: one demo block on the homepage renders a scene list and a re-render row. No other page in the set carries a scene, version or edit component, and nothing in the type, colour or shape system refers to it.
02

The register undersells the buyer

A marker headline face at weight 800, bright accents and sticker shadows set an expectation of a light consumer tool. The site addresses five audiences in that one register: developers, founders, teachers, course creators and support teams. For teachers and course creators the tone is an asset. For the developer and founder pages, which open on "You shipped it. Now explain it, from your terminal." and sell an API, an MCP server and a CLI, the register works against the copy.

Evidence: five audience pages under /for/, each with its own headline and job to be done, all rendered in one visual register. Which segment produces the most revenue is not something we can see from outside, so this is an argument about fit, not about revenue.
03

The palette is decorative, not functional

Six accents exist. None has a defined job, and seven of the nine tokens cannot carry body text on the site's own background. The result is a system with no way to signal state, hierarchy or meaning through colour, so colour becomes noise rather than information. Section 05 has the numbers.

Evidence: computed contrast ratios, section 05. Violet passes at 4.59 on warm paper. Sunny fails at 1.45.
04

The signature device is now a template

Inked outlines with hard offset shadows are a widely used web style, and in our judgement common enough now that they read as a theme rather than as a specific brand. We cannot measure how widespread it is, so treat this one as a designer's read rather than a measurement. What is measurable is that the device does most of the identity's work here.

Evidence: the shadow, border and radius rules are the only consistently applied visual signature in the system.
05

The brand ignores its own best asset

The product output is genuinely good: line art drawing itself, timed to narration, on a clean board. The site frames those videos in decorated containers that compete with them. An identity built outward from the output, rather than around it, would both look better and argue for the product at the same time.

Evidence: every video on the site sits inside a bordered, shadowed panel with its own visual personality.

06 / What to keep

An overhaul is not a reset

There is real equity here, and discarding it would cost more than it gains.

  • 01Violet. It is the one token on every page, it passes contrast, and it is recognisable. It should survive, with a defined job rather than a decorative one.
  • 02The light board. Drawn dark line art needs a light surface. Whatever the palette becomes, the board stays legible behind the output.
  • 03Hand-made character. The product draws. The brand should feel made by a person. The question is whether that comes from a novelty typeface or from the artwork, texture and detailing instead.
  • 04The honest voice. Sentence case, exact numbers, limits stated out loud, a competitor comparison that concedes. That is a genuine differentiator and it is free to keep.
  • 05The refusals. Six minutes maximum, no talking heads, no stock footage. Design should dramatise those decisions rather than hide them.

08 / Directions explored

Three built, not described

Each is a working page rather than a mood board, so the argument can be had against something real.

Direction A

Two-ink press

Risograph printing on bone stock. Overprint, misregistration, halftone fields, poster type. Warm and physical, strongest for the education half.

Open direction A
Direction B

Night press

The same print logic on deep blue stock with fluorescent ink. The video panel becomes the only light source. Reads more studio, more technical.

Open direction B
Direction C, recommended

The page is the demo

The page behaves like a Sketchie video: it draws itself scene by scene, a playhead tracks position, one scene re-renders on demand while the others stay put, and it ends on a retrieval beat.

Open direction C

Why C is the recommendation

A and B are strong visual systems, but they are skins: the same argument in a new costume. C is the only one that answers objective two, because the differentiator is not described in copy, it is demonstrated by the page itself. It is also the only one that would be hard for a competitor to copy, since the behaviour depends on actually having an editable scene graph.

Its palette is the open question. Near-black with an acid accent is one expression of the idea, not the idea itself, and it can carry a warmer or more conservative treatment without losing the mechanic.

11 / What we do not promise

Stated plainly

We will commit to: every text colour passing WCAG AA on its background, a palette where each colour has a stated role, a visual device that expresses the scene model, motion that honours reduced-motion settings, a working page rather than a mood board at each phase, and a token file the team can apply without us.

We will not promise: a conversion increase, a signup lift, a traffic change, or that a new identity fixes positioning. Design can make a true claim legible. It cannot make a claim true, and it cannot substitute for the product decisions underneath it.

The honest summary. The accessibility failures are worth fixing whatever you decide about the brand. The rest of this report is an argument that the identity is costing credibility with the half of the audience that pays most, and that the product's one real differentiator deserves to be visible.
