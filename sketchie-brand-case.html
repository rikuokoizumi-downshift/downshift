Design audit and revamp proposal

The brand is sound. The page it is wearing is not organised.

Sketchie does not need a new identity. The violet, the warm board, the dotted paper and the honest voice are all working. What is not working is the thing underneath them: the page renders twenty-one text sizes, its top heading sits four pixels from its section headings, three of its headings rank below its own body copy, and the five audiences it sells to are never routed anywhere above the footer. That is a revamp, not a rebrand.

01 / The case in short

Three reasons to revamp, none of them taste

Design arguments usually come down to preference, which is why they stall. These three were taken off the running site at a 1440px desktop width on 2026-10-16, and anyone can repeat them in a browser console.

21Distinct font sizes rendering visible text on the homepage. Twelve of them sit between 11 and 17px, in half-pixel steps.
4pxThe gap between the page’s h1 at 48px and its seven h2s at 44px. Both are weight 800 in the same face.
7 of 9Palette colours that cannot carry body text on the site’s own background. Arithmetic, not opinion.

One, the type scale cannot signal rank. Twenty-one sizes is not a hierarchy, it is a list. Half a pixel is not a step a reader can perceive, so size stops carrying meaning and the page flattens. The headline and the section headings are four pixels apart, and h4 renders at 15.5px while four of the six paragraph sizes are larger than that — a heading ranking below the text it introduces.

Two, the order asks for a decision before it earns one. Six content bands run between the hero and the close. None of them is an audience band. Sketchie names five buyers — developers, founders, teachers, course creators, support teams — gives each a page, and then routes to them only from the footer, after pricing and the FAQ.

Three, colour carries no information. Six accents exist with no assigned jobs, and seven of nine tokens fail contrast for body text. A palette that cannot be used for text or state is decoration, which is why the page leans entirely on size and weight it does not have to spare.

What this is not. This is not a case for a new identity. Section 06 lists what the revamp keeps, and it is most of the brand.

Measured against judged

Measured: the rendered size, weight and family of every text element, the token values, contrast ratios computed from the published hex values, font loading status, the homepage heading sequence, and the page inventory.

Judged: that a marker face carrying every heading sets the maturity level of the brand, that the offset-shadow device now reads as a theme rather than a signature, and that accessibility matters commercially in education. These are a designer’s read, labelled as such wherever they appear.

Not known: which audience segment produces the most revenue, and how any of this affects conversion. That needs analytics we have not been given, and no claim in this report depends on it.

04 / Why the site needs revamping

Five arguments, with the evidence attached

01

The type scale cannot signal rank

Hierarchy is how a reader decides what to read. It is carried by size, weight and space, and on this page size has been spent without being spent on anything. Twenty-one sizes render text. Twelve of them crowd into the 11 to 17px band, mostly half a pixel apart, which is below the threshold at which a difference reads as a difference. The levels that should be far apart are close instead: the single h1 is 48px and the seven h2s are 44px, both weight 800 in the same face, so the page has a headline only in the markup. Below that, h4 renders at 15.5px while paragraphs run up to 20px, which inverts the relationship entirely.

Evidence: computed styles, homepage at 1440px, 2026-10-16. 21 distinct sizes; 12 in the 11 to 17px band; h1 48 / h2 44 / h3 21 / h4 15.5; six paragraph sizes at 13, 14.5, 17, 18, 19 and 20.
02

The page never routes the five audiences

Sketchie names five buyers and writes a dedicated page for each: developers, founders, teachers, course creators and support teams. The homepage never offers that choice. Six content bands run between the hero and the close, and none of them is an audience band, so the only route to a page written for the visitor is a footer link reached after pricing and the FAQ. A reader who needs to see themselves to keep reading has nowhere to look.

Evidence: homepage heading sequence, 2026-10-16. In order: “Prompt in. Video out”, “Fix one scene. Not the whole video.”, “Built different where it counts.”, “We optimized for whether they remember it tomorrow.”, “Four plans. Start free with 2 videos.”, “The honest FAQ.”, then the close. The five segments appear under a footer heading reading “Who it’s for”.
03

Colour carries no information

Six accents exist. None has a defined job, and seven of the nine tokens cannot carry body text on the site’s own background. The consequence is practical rather than aesthetic: with colour unavailable for state, rank or meaning, the page has only size and weight left to work with, and section 04.01 shows those are already exhausted. Fixing the palette gives the hierarchy a second channel.

Evidence: computed contrast ratios, section 05. Violet passes at 4.59 on warm paper. Sunny fails at 1.45.
04

The differentiator appears once and never returns

Sketchie’s defensible claim is that a video is a set of addressable scenes, so the second version costs a scene instead of a rebuild. The homepage does show this, once, as a demo block with a scene list and a re-render row. It is the strongest thing on the page and it is placed where a skimming reader meets it after the mechanics, then never again. This is a placement problem, not an identity problem: the asset exists and is good, it is simply used once.

Evidence: one demo block on the homepage renders a scene list and a re-render row. No other page in the set carries a scene, version or edit component.
05

The system has drifted from its own definition

A third typeface, Caveat, is declared as --annotation and rendered by nothing, so the browser never requests the file. Six accents are defined with no assigned roles. Body copy runs at six sizes. None of these is a catastrophe on its own, and together they are the ordinary drift any site accumulates while shipping quickly. They are also exactly what a revamp is for: the definitions and the rendered page have come apart, and bringing them back together is cheaper than it looks.

Evidence: --annotation declared in the live stylesheet; no element renders in Caveat on any page checked; six accent tokens with no semantic role; six paragraph sizes in use.

Why this belongs in a revamp. None of these ratios requires a new identity to fix. Violet and deep violet already pass, so the action colour is settled; the work is assigning roles to the rest and keeping them away from text. Sketchie also markets directly to teachers and schools, and if accessibility is ever asked about, a palette that cannot carry body text is an awkward answer. How often that question comes up in education buying is not something we have measured, so treat that part as judgement. The ratios are arithmetic, which is what makes this the easiest argument to settle internally.

06 / What the revamp keeps

Most of the brand, deliberately

This matters more than the list of changes. A revamp that quietly becomes a rebrand costs the equity it was supposed to protect, so here is what does not move.

  • 01Violet, and the deep violet with it. On every page, passes contrast, recognisable. It stays, and it gains a defined job instead of a decorative one.
  • 02The warm board. #fbf8f3 paper, white panels, #1e1e28 ink. Drawn dark line art needs a light surface, and this is the right one.
  • 03The dotted paper. 1.3px dots at 26px. Kept exactly, and used in fewer, more deliberate places so it reads as a surface rather than a wallpaper.
  • 04The inked edge and the offset shadow. 2px borders, 4px 5px 0 shadows. This is the signature and the revamp keeps it rather than replacing it.
  • 05Inter for body. Already the right choice. It simply stops being asked to work at six sizes.
  • 06The honest voice. Sentence case, exact numbers, limits stated out loud, a competitor comparison that concedes. A genuine differentiator, and free to keep.
  • 07The refusals. Six minutes maximum, no talking heads, no stock footage. The revamp gives them a band of their own rather than hiding them.
  • 08The motion feel. 0.12s hover lift, shadow grow. Physical and snappy. Untouched.

Open it anyway

Why a revamp rather than a rebrand

Three reasons, in order of weight. The equity is real and is concentrated in things that are cheap to keep: a violet that passes contrast, a warm board that suits drawn output, and a voice that concedes to a competitor in public. Second, none of the five problems in section 04 is caused by the identity — they are caused by the scale, the order and the roles, all of which can be fixed without touching a single brand asset. Third, a rebrand carries a cost this does not: every page, every social image, every doc shell and every piece of collateral has to follow, and the argument for paying that is not in the measurements.

One honest flag. The revamp page uses a different display face from the live site, and that is the one change in it that goes beyond reorganising. The hierarchy problems in section 04 can be fixed with Shantell Sans kept in place, and we can rebuild the page that way on request. Section 10 carries it as a decision rather than a recommendation.

11 / What we do not promise

Stated plainly

We will commit to: a type scale of nine steps, every adjacent pair at least 12 per cent apart, with no heading smaller than the body copy in its own component, every text colour passing WCAG AA on its background, every colour carrying a stated role, the band order in section 07, motion that honours reduced-motion settings, a working page rather than a mood board at each phase, and a token file the team can apply without us.

We will not promise: a conversion increase, a signup lift, a traffic change, or that reorganising a page fixes positioning. Design can make a true claim legible. It cannot make a claim true, and it cannot substitute for the product decisions underneath it.

The honest summary. Two of the findings here are worth fixing whatever you decide about everything else: the contrast failures, and a heading level that renders smaller than the paragraphs beneath it. The rest of this report argues that the brand is not the problem, the order and the scale are, and that both can be fixed without spending the equity.
