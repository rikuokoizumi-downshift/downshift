Site map and optimisation report

The structure of sketchie.ai, and what it costs today.

A read of the live site as crawlers and readers encounter it, the defects that structure is currently producing, a proposed map, and a phased route to it that does not put existing rankings at risk.

01 / Summary

Three findings worth acting on

The site is well built and the content is strong. The problems are structural, and they are mostly invisible from inside the design: they show up in how the site is crawled, how locales resolve, and which pages exist at all.

404sketchie.ai/sitemap.xml does not resolve. The sitemap lives at /sitemap-index.xml, which is not the path tools try first.
5 pagesThe audience pages under /for/ have no translated versions and no index page. /ja/for/developers/ returns 404.
1,113URLs in the sitemap: 78 locale prefixes across 14 paths, plus 21 root paths.

One, discovery is leaking. The conventional sitemap path 404s, and the five audience pages sit outside the hreflang cluster entirely, which makes them less likely to be chosen as canonical.

Two, the locale system contradicts Google's own guidance. The site automatically redirects visitors by location. Google's documentation says to avoid that, because it can stop users and crawlers from seeing all versions.

Three, two pages a buyer looks for do not exist. /blog/ and /contact/ both return 404.

02 / What we checked

Method, and the limits of it

Everything in section 04 was requested live on 2026-10-15 and read from the response, not inferred. Status codes were checked individually. The sitemap was parsed and counted. Hreflang annotations were read from the served HTML.

  • 01Done: live status checks, sitemap parse and count, hreflang audit on the homepage, a locale page and an audience page, robots directives, and a read of the public page set.
  • 02Not done yet: a full crawl of all 1,113 URLs, which needs a licensed crawler. Internal link graph, orphan detection and crawl depth all depend on it.
  • 03Not available: analytics and Search Console. Without them, nothing here can be ranked by traffic or revenue impact, so the priorities in section 08 are based on severity and effort alone.

One number to reconcile. The sitemap contains 78 locale prefixes. The footer language picker appears to offer closer to 97. One of those counts is wrong, and a full crawl settles it. We have not assumed either.

07 / Localisation

The locale axis needs governing, not expanding

  • 01Stop automatic redirection. Detect, then offer. A banner or a switcher respects the URL the visitor asked for, which is what Google's guidance recommends and what a person linking to a specific page expects.
  • 02Make the switcher stay on the page. Today it sends a reader on /for/developers/ to a locale homepage. It should go to the same page in the new locale, or be hidden where no translation exists.
  • 03Add reciprocal hreflang everywhere, including x-default. Each version must list itself and all others, or the annotations are ignored.
  • 04Tier the locales. 78 prefixes across 14 paths is a maintenance surface, not a market strategy. Tiering needs demand data, which is in the analytics we have not seen.
  • 05Either translate the /for/ pages or exclude them deliberately. The current state, where they exist in one locale only and carry no alternates, is the worst of both.

09 / How we would validate

Before the structure ships, not after

A proposed hierarchy is a hypothesis. Two cheap tests settle most disagreements, and both run without any visual design.

  • 01Tree test the proposed structure with 30 to 50 participants, measuring task success, which wrong branch was taken, and directness. Run two label variants if the audience-page naming is contested.
  • 02Open card sort with about 15 participants, but only if the top level is genuinely in dispute. At 15 participants the result correlates about 0.90 with a full sample.
  • 03Crawl diff before and after each phase, so a structural change that orphans a page is caught before release rather than in a ranking report.

On click depth: we will not promise a three-click rule. It is not supported by evidence, and the research that is often cited for it found no worsening of drop-off past three clicks. We optimise for information scent, hub pages and wayfinding instead.

11 / What we do not promise

Stated plainly, before anyone asks

We will commit to: a one to one redirect map with no 404 for any URL that has clicks or inbound links, redirect chains of three hops or fewer, self-referencing canonicals, reciprocal hreflang including x-default, a crawl diff before each release, a monitoring window at 7, 30 and 90 days, and a rollback plan per phase.

We will not promise: that rankings are retained, that there is no traffic dip during transition, any traffic or conversion percentage, a reindexing timeline, or a maximum click depth. Movement during a restructure is normal, and anyone promising otherwise is guessing.

Redirects stay for at least a year. That is Google's own guidance for a move with URL changes, and it is the single most common thing teams remove too early.
